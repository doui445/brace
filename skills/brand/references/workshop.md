# Framing workshop

Read by `SKILL.md` at the phase `framing` (`brand_stage: brief`, the
proposal accepted). The workshop frames the mission. Its outcome is the
validated `BRIEF.md`.

## Before the first question

1. Open it as a meeting (`SKILL.md`, a meeting, part 1): what we are going
   to do today, in her words (for example, that she has read the project,
   will show what she understood, and only ask what she could not find).
2. **Read the project first**: its front page, the code and its comments
   when there is code, a site if there is one, the notes, `STATUS.md`,
   `docs/brace/agents/` if earlier reports exist, and the client's own
   brand documents (`- existing:` lines). What already exists is material,
   never an answer: quote it and ask whether it still holds.
3. Write a **pre-filled brief** from what you read, under the
   `brand.brief.*` headings, as a client document (`references/documents.md`,
   file name `brief`, phase `framing`), render it and show it. No
   "assumption" tags in it, and no "pre-filled" in its title: its
   introduction (`brand.brief.intro`) says it was pre-filled and invites
   the client to say what does not sound like them. You may keep, for
   yourself, which lines you read and which you inferred; the client's
   document does not badge them.

Profile: propose product or personal brand from the context, with your
recommendation, as the first line of the brief, and write
`brand_profile` once the client agrees. A visual element the client keeps
(`- existing: visual`) is written in the brief as a constraint.

## The questions

Only for the headings the project could not answer, asked together, each
with an example, the possible answers as selectable choices and your
recommendation. Never an open question on its own.

1. **The project**: what it does, in one sentence a stranger understands.
   Offer two phrasings drawn from its front page.
2. **What the brand must achieve**: users, clients, partners, hiring,
   visibility. Offer the two that fit the project.
3. **Who it is for**: offer two or three profiles drawn from the project,
   with the one you would start with.
4. **Competitors you know**: list the ones you found; ask which to add or
   drop. "I don't know" is fine: our analyst will look.
5. **The name**: keep it, find one, or keep it as a working name. Say what
   each choice starts.
6. **What you like**: two or three brands the client admires, in any
   field, and what they like about them. Offer examples of very different
   stances (calm and plain, bold and loud, warm and close) to point at.
7. **The feeling to leave**: one emotion, not a list. Offer three that fit
   the project, with your recommendation.
8. **What you never want**: a tone, a word, a cliche of the market. Offer
   the cliches you spotted.
9. **The brand's languages**, always asked, in one question: the brand's
   **main language** (what the public reads), named as such, pre-filled
   with the language of the project's front page or public texts; and any
   **secondary languages**, now or later (`SKILL.md`, languages). The
   working language was settled at the first contact and is not asked
   again.

The client does not know an answer: write "to fill in". The tastes and the
rejections are kept: the presentation weighs them against the target.

For a personal brand, also run the entourage test from
`references/personal.md`.

## The interview guide

The agency prepares an interview guide the client can run with 3 to 5
real people from the target, whenever they can, and says what to do with
it, in full sentences: what it is (short conversations about real past
moments), what it takes (twenty minutes each), what it brings (the
personas speak with real words, not ours). It never holds anything up: our
team goes ahead without it.

> Ask for real past stories, not predictions: "Tell me about the last time
> you...". Ask what they were trying to get done when they reached for a
> product like this. Write down their exact words.

What they bring back later feeds the personas. Never invent answers in its
place.

## Closing the workshop

1. Re-render the completed brief and get a yes.
2. Write `docs/brace/brand/BRIEF.md` (the `brand.brief.*` headings) and the
   `SESSIONS.md` entry; a new `- brand:` line if the name changed.
3. Close the meeting (`SKILL.md`, closing a meeting): the questions the
   next phase needs (`references/backstage.md`), the minutes, the next
   appointment.
4. Save (`SKILL.md`, `## Saving`).
5. Our team gets to work: set `brand_stage: backstage` and start
   `references/backstage.md`.
