# Workshop

Read by `SKILL.md` at the first session (`brand_stage: brief`). The
workshop frames the mission. It ends on a validated `BRIEF.md`.

## Before the first question

1. Say what is going to happen (`SKILL.md`, `## Starting`): the five
   steps, the time, what needs the user, the measured cost, and that they
   can stop whenever they like.
2. **Read the project first**: `README.md`, the code and its comments, a
   site or landing page if the repo has one, the notes, `STATUS.md`,
   `docs/brace/agents/` if earlier reports exist. What already exists is
   material, never an answer: quote it and ask whether it still holds.
3. Write a **pre-filled brief** from what you read, under the
   `brand.brief.*` headings, and show it first, rendered as a page (a
   `slide doc` in `presentations/draft.html`, see
   `references/presentation.md`), never as Markdown. Mark each line you
   inferred as an assumption.

Profile: propose product or personal brand from the context, with your
recommendation, as the first line of the brief, and write
`brand_profile` once the user agrees.

## The questions

Only for the headings the project could not answer, one at a time. Each
question comes with an example, two or three possible answers, and your
recommendation. Never an open question on its own.

1. **The project**: what it does, in one sentence a stranger understands.
   Offer two phrasings drawn from the README.
2. **What the brand must achieve**: users, clients, partners, hiring,
   visibility. Offer the two that fit the project.
3. **Who it is for**: offer two or three profiles drawn from the project,
   with the one you would start with.
4. **Competitors you know**: list the ones you found; ask which to add or
   drop. "I don't know" is fine: the analyst will look.
5. **The name**: keep it, find one, or keep it as a working name. Say what
   each choice starts.
6. **What you like**: two or three brands the user admires, in any field,
   and what they like about them. Offer examples of very different stances
   (calm and plain, bold and loud, warm and close) to point at.
7. **The feeling to leave**: one emotion, not a list. Offer three that fit
   the project, with your recommendation.
8. **What you never want**: a tone, a word, a cliche of the market. Offer
   the cliches you spotted.
9. **The languages**, always asked: the working language and the brand's language
   (`SKILL.md`, `## Languages`), in one question, pre-filled: we work in
   the language the user writes in, the brand speaks the language of the
   README or the public texts. Offer that pairing and one or two others
   (both the same, the other way round), with your recommendation.

The user does not know an answer: write "to fill in", or, when the
presentation can settle it, an assumption marked as one ("Assumption:
freelancers first; to check at the presentation"). The tastes and the
rejections are kept: the presentation weighs them against the target.

For a personal brand, also run the entourage test from
`references/personal.md`.

## The interview guide

Offer, once, an interview guide the user can run with 3 to 5 real people
from the target, whenever they can:

> Ask for real past stories, not predictions: "Tell me about the last time
> you...". Ask what they were trying to get done when they reached for a
> product like this. Write down their exact words.

What they bring back later feeds the personas. Never invent answers in its
place.

## Closing the workshop

1. Show the completed brief, rendered, and get a yes.
2. Write `docs/brace/brand/BRIEF.md` (the `brand.brief.*` headings, the
   assumptions under their own heading).
3. Append the first entry to `docs/brace/brand/SESSIONS.md`
   (`brand.sessions.*`): decided, waiting, next.
4. Say: "I'm on it. The team works in the background; you can do something
   else, here or in another session. I will tell you when the presentation
   is ready." Say it is saved and the user can stop here.
5. Set `brand_stage: backstage` and start `references/backstage.md`.
