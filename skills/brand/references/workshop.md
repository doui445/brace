# Framing workshop

Read by `SKILL.md` at meeting 1 (`brand_stage: brief`, the proposal
accepted). The workshop frames the mission. Its minutes are the validated
`BRIEF.md`.

## Before the first question

1. Open it as a meeting: "Let's start the framing workshop. I have read
   your project; I will show you what I understood, and only ask what I
   could not find."
2. **Read the project first**: its front page, the code and its comments
   when there is code, a site if there is one, the notes, `STATUS.md`,
   `docs/brace/agents/` if earlier reports exist. What already exists is
   material, never an answer: quote it and ask whether it still holds.
3. Write a **pre-filled brief** from what you read, under the
   `brand.brief.*` headings, as a client document (`references/documents.md`,
   file name `brief`, meeting 1), render it and show it. Mark each line you
   inferred as an assumption.

Profile: propose product or personal brand from the context, with your
recommendation, as the first line of the brief, and write
`brand_profile` once the client agrees.

## The questions

Only for the headings the project could not answer, one at a time. Each
question comes with an example, two or three possible answers, and your
recommendation. Never an open question on its own.

1. **The project**: what it does, in one sentence a stranger understands.
   Offer two phrasings drawn from its front page.
2. **What the brand must achieve**: users, clients, partners, hiring,
   visibility. Offer the two that fit the project.
3. **Who it is for**: offer two or three profiles drawn from the project,
   with the one you would start with.
4. **Competitors you know**: list the ones you found; ask which to add or
   drop. "I don't know" is fine: the analyst will look.
5. **The name**: keep it, find one, or keep it as a working name. Say what
   each choice starts.
6. **What you like**: two or three brands the client admires, in any
   field, and what they like about them. Offer examples of very different
   stances (calm and plain, bold and loud, warm and close) to point at.
7. **The feeling to leave**: one emotion, not a list. Offer three that fit
   the project, with your recommendation.
8. **What you never want**: a tone, a word, a cliche of the market. Offer
   the cliches you spotted.
9. **The languages**, always asked: the working language and the brand's
   language (`SKILL.md`, `## Languages`), in one question, pre-filled: we
   work in the language the client writes in, the brand speaks the
   language of the project's front page or public texts. Offer that
   pairing and one or two others (both the same, the other way round),
   with your recommendation.

The client does not know an answer: write "to fill in", or, when the
presentation can settle it, an assumption marked as one ("Assumption:
freelancers first; to check at the presentation"). The tastes and the
rejections are kept: the presentation weighs them against the target.

For a personal brand, also run the entourage test from
`references/personal.md`.

## The interview guide

Offer, once, an interview guide the client can run with 3 to 5 real people
from the target, whenever they can: what it is (short conversations about
real past moments), what it takes (twenty minutes each), what it brings
(the personas speak with real words, not ours).

> Ask for real past stories, not predictions: "Tell me about the last time
> you...". Ask what they were trying to get done when they reached for a
> product like this. Write down their exact words.

What they bring back later feeds the personas. Never invent answers in its
place.

## Closing the workshop

1. Re-render the completed brief and get a yes: these are the workshop's
   minutes.
2. Write `docs/brace/brand/BRIEF.md` (the `brand.brief.*` headings, the
   assumptions under their own heading) and the `SESSIONS.md` entry.
3. Set the next appointment (`SKILL.md`, the closing of a meeting).
4. Save (`SKILL.md`, `## Saving`).
5. "The team gets to work..." Set `brand_stage: backstage` and start
   `references/backstage.md`.
