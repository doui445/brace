# Creative routes

Read by `SKILL.md` before and at the creative meeting (`creation`,
`brand_stage: presentation`). The client chooses the look and the voice
of their brand. A route is one idea, its words and its look together; on
words only when the look is out of scope (the client keeps their logo,
or someone else does the look).

## Before the meeting (backstage)

Built on the validated platform, the name and the client's success
criteria (`references/backstage.md`, before the creation):

1. **`art-director`** explores wide (more ideas than will be shown, six
   say), each named by an adjective, and keeps two or three that really
   differ and serve the platform. For each: the idea, the mood, the
   palette, the type pair, the direction of the logo, and why. It follows
   the craft (`references/visual.md`).
2. **`copywriter`**, with it: each route's tagline, one-liner and tone.
3. **`designer`**: each route's logo sketch as a clean SVG, and its palette
   checked, in the tool mode chosen at the first contact
   (`references/tools.md`); in code otherwise.
4. **`brand`** builds the creative deck; **`creative-director`** reviews
   the whole, look included (the twelve criteria, the logo, the numbers,
   equal treatment).

Generated images are for exploring only: they go to `brand/sources/`
(out of git, `render.py sources`), never into a deliverable. In code mode
the mood has no image: references, flat colours, type specimens, simple
SVG patterns.

## The creative deck

Every route is developed **the same way, on the same supports**, so the
client compares like with like:

1. `cover`: the client's brief in a sentence and **their success
   criteria**, so they judge the routes by them.
2. One slide for the platform, in short.
3. For each route, in this order: `route` (the adjective and the idea,
   the tagline and the tone), `mood`, `palette`, `type`, `logo` (the logo
   revealed on its own, with its reason), then `context` (the route on the
   client's **real** supports: their project's front page, their site,
   their profiles, dressed in the route's look). A rebrand adds the
   current look next to the new one.
4. `compare` across the routes, then `reco`: our recommendation, weighed
   against the criteria, and the next step (the refinement, or the
   delivery when the look is out of scope).

Each colour is explained by what it does for this brand (its link to the
platform, how it stands apart from the competitors, its fit with the
category and the audience, its use), never by a universal symbolism.

## The meeting

The five parts of `SKILL.md` (a meeting). In the discussion, propose:
give the recommendation and why in a sentence, then the choice as
selectable options (route 1, route 2, a mix, another proposal). The
client says what they keep or change ("this mark, thinner"; "the other
route's green"): small changes are settled now; no route suits is a new
proposal (`references/adjustments.md`).

Close the meeting: the minutes (the route chosen, what is kept and
changed, the routes set aside and why, also in
`docs/brace/brand/DECISIONS.md`), the questions for the next phase, the
next appointment: the refinement when the look is in scope, else the
delivery. Set `brand_stage: adjustments` (refinement) or
`brand_stage: delivery`.
